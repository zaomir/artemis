# A solar roof and a stone shell: a home that holds its own warmth

On the northern slope of La Quinta, each of the twenty Artemis homes will work to cut its own electricity bill: solar panels flush with the roof, smart climate and lighting, deep windows, a stone shell and a shared heated pool. For La Quinta, the European Commission's PVGIS tool gives about 1,578 kWh a year per kilowatt of panels at the optimal tilt of 23°, against about 1,771 kWh on average across the Canaries at the optimal tilt of 21°. Artemis panels lie flat, flush with the roof, and for them PVGIS gives about 1,490 kWh. We base our figures on our own location and our own installation.

### A typical island home

**Electricity.** All of it from the grid, at full price. Air conditioning in summer, a heater on damp winter evenings, a water heater all year round. In a Tenerife installer's example, the bill without panels is 120 € a month.

**Hot water.** An electric water heater that heats water even when nobody is home.

**The pool in winter.** Cold. Or heated at such a cost that it is easier to leave it off.

**An empty home.** The owner leaves, the home gets cold and damp, and it takes days to warm up again on their return.

**Thin windows, thin walls.** Heat escapes in winter and the heat gets in during summer. The bill rises both ways.

### An Artemis home

**Solar panels flush with the roof.** Dark panels sit flush with the flat roof, finished with a slim bronze edge. From the street the home looks clean, while the roof will be working throughout the daylight hours.

**Smart climate and lighting.** The home keeps its own temperature and switches off unnecessary lights. The system's job is to stop the home getting cold and damp while the owner is away, and to have it warm when they arrive.

**Deep windows.** Tall windows in slim bronze frames are set deep into the wall. In summer the reveal keeps the shade; in winter the glass lets in the low sun. Plenty of light, little overheating.

**A stone shell.** A plinth of dark volcanic stone, stone terraces, light lime render. Stone warms up slowly and cools down slowly: the home keeps a steady temperature without sudden swings.

**A shared heated pool.** Evenings in the north can be cool, and heating keeps the pool usable all year round, even in January.

### What the sun delivers in the Canaries

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Output of 1 kW of panels, Canaries average, optimal tilt 21° | about 1,771 kWh a year |
| Output of 1 kW of panels, La Quinta, PVGIS, optimal tilt 23° | about 1,578 kWh a year |
| Output of 1 kW of panels flush with the roof, La Quinta, PVGIS | about 1,490 kWh a year |
| Home installation, Spain-wide guide | 3 to 4 kilowatts |
| Annual consumption it is sized for, same guide | 2,500–4,500 kWh |
| Installer's example: savings from one installation | 600–1,000 € a year |
| Example: bill without panels in the same calculation | 120 € a month, 120 × 12 = 1,440 € a year |

Installation size and consumption come from a Spain-wide guide to home installations, not from island data. The savings and the bill are one installer's example, not an average. For La Quinta itself, the figure is a PVGIS calculation based on satellite data for 2005–2023. A fair comparison uses the optimal tilt for each location (23° and 21°): 1,578 against 1,771 kWh. A flat installation, as on the Artemis roofs, gives less: about 1,490.

The neighbouring La Quinta 2 project has also been announced with solar panels.

### Why this matters for renting

The Artemis dividend is calculated from long-term rental after costs: the home is let long term, to a tenant for a year or more. A long-term tenant in the north sees the electricity bill just as you do. A home that is warm in winter without a heater, with a lower electricity bill and a heated pool, is easier to let and makes it easier to keep a tenant for another year. Fewer empty months between tenants means steadier rent after costs.

### Why this matters for the price per metre

A new home with solar on the roof and a stone shell costs more than an old flat of the same size and is cheaper to run. The neighbouring La Quinta new builds were offered at 3,360–3,870 € per metre; both projects have sold out. A metre bought as an Artemis developer company share costs ≈2,900–3,100 €/m² if you enter from the queue and ≈3,700–3,900 €/m² by completion: the home's energy features are included in the price per metre.

### Why this matters for shareholders

Joining the queue is free. We are now choosing the site in La Quinta, Santa Úrsula, and discussing the options with queue members. We take no money before the site is chosen: the investment contract arrives once most of the queue approves the site. Under the contract you buy developer company shares at the price of the current stage: one share equals one m² of the home's total built area, excluding common areas. Pay in full upfront for a 10% discount on the entry price, in instalments over six months — six equal payments with a 5% discount — or in instalments over a year with no discount. A report every week of construction. Once the home is completed, you receive a dividend in euros on every share from long-term rental after costs, estimated and before tax, or exchange your shares for the metres of your own residence.

The dividend is rent minus costs. The less the homes spend on electricity, water and the pool, the more of the rent is left. Itemised costs come to 46.3% of rent; the expected dividend is 2.5–3.5% a year before tax at the queue entry price. The largest item is community fees, at 21.0%: they cover the shared heated pool and upkeep of the grounds. The less the shared systems draw from the grid, the lower this item.

This is a forecast, not a guarantee: the dividend may be lower or may not be paid, and the share price may fall. Tax consequences depend on your personal situation.

### The north and the sun

On the northern slope the sun is gentler than in the south, which is why it is green here and never too hot. There is enough light for panels in the Canaries all year round. By day the roof will be working; in the evening the stone will keep the home warm and the heating will keep the pool warm.
