# La Quinta, Santa Úrsula: the north, where Tenerife lives

La Quinta is a residential area of Santa Úrsula on Tenerife's green northern slope. Puerto de la Cruz is 8 km away, 10–15 minutes by car; Tenerife North airport is 15–20 minutes; Playa del Ancón is about 3 km. This is where we are choosing the site for the twenty Artemis residences. A metre bought as a developer company share costs ≈2,900–3,100 €/m² if you enter from the queue and ≈3,700–3,900 €/m² by completion.

### Where it is on the map

Santa Úrsula lies between Puerto de la Cruz and the island's capital. La Quinta is its coastal part below the TF-5 motorway, on a slope facing the Atlantic. Exit 29 off the TF-5 leads to the area.

### North versus south

**The south**: beach, heat, yellow dust and a resort crowd that changes every week. People fly there for a holiday.

**The north**: greenery, mild days without scorching heat, banana terraces, laurel forest on the slopes and the summit of Teide above the clouds. People come here to live: the town, the capital and the airport are all close. The community is Western European.

If you have already looked at the south, you know what January looks like there: the same sunbeds and the same people with suitcases. In the north, January is a green slope with a "sea of clouds" above it at around 800–1,500 metres, and warm water in a heated pool in the evening.

### What's nearby

- **Puerto de la Cruz.** An old town by the ocean, a seafront promenade, Playa Jardín with dark volcanic sand, the Lago Martiánez pools and Loro Parque.
- **Santa Úrsula beaches.** Playa del Ancón and, along the coastal path, Los Patos and Bollullo.
- **Mirador de La Quinta.** A municipal viewpoint above the coast.
- **Santa Cruz.** The island's capital: offices, hospitals, theatres, the port.
- **La Orotava.** A historic town and valley next door.

### La Quinta as a neighbourhood

La Quinta is a residential area, not a resort zone. People live here all year round rather than staying for a week.

For your home, this means two things. Your neighbours are people who live here permanently, not a weekly turnover. And the long-term rental that the Artemis shareholder dividend is calculated from relies on people who need the town, the capital and the airport nearby — residents, not the tourist season.

The twenty Artemis homes are an intimate complex, not a tower block. One architectural language, Canary Stone: warm lime render, a plinth of dark volcanic stone, flat roofs with solar panels, tall windows in bronze frames, stone terraces.

### Why prices in the north are rising

The north is no longer "the side of the island you visit on an excursion". All housing in Santa Úrsula is up 13.7% over the last year, according to Fotocasa.

### Renting in the north

Long-term rent in Santa Úrsula is about 12 € per metre a month according to Fotocasa, and 15.04 € for apartments according to Engel & Völkers. In Puerto de la Cruz it is about 17 €. Gross rental yield in Santa Úrsula, according to Mesurae, is 5.8%. For a 72-metre home at 12–15 € per metre, that is 864–1,080 € a month. Long-term rental is exactly what the Artemis shareholder dividend is calculated from.

### How entry works

Joining the queue is free. We are now choosing the site in La Quinta, Santa Úrsula, and discussing the options with queue members. We take no money before the site is chosen: the investment contract arrives once most of the queue approves the site. Under the contract you buy developer company shares at the price of the current stage: one share equals one m² of the home's total built area, excluding common areas. Pay in full upfront for a 10% discount on the entry price, in instalments over six months — six equal payments with a 5% discount — or in instalments over a year with no discount. A report every week of construction. Once the home is completed, you receive a dividend in euros on every share from long-term rental after costs, estimated and before tax, or exchange your shares for the metres of your own residence.
