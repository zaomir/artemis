A solar roof, a stone outline: a house that holds its own warmth

On the northern slope at La Quinta, each of the twenty Artemis houses will work towards its own electricity bill: solar panels flush with the roof plane, smart climate and lighting, deep windows, a stone outline and a shared heated pool. For La Quinta the European Commission's PVGIS gives about 1 578 kWh a year per kilowatt of panels at an optimal tilt of 23°, against about 1 771 kWh averaged across the Canaries at an optimal tilt of 21°. The Artemis panels lie flat, in the roof plane, and for those PVGIS gives about 1 490 kWh. We count on our own site and our own installation.

An ordinary house on the island

Electricity. All from the grid, at full price. Air conditioning in summer, a heater on damp winter evenings, a boiler all year round. In a Tenerife installer's calculation, an example bill without panels is 120 € a month.

Hot water. An electric boiler that heats water even when nobody is in the house.

The pool in winter. Cold. Or heated at a price that makes it easier not to switch it on.

An empty house. The owner leaves, the house cools and gets damp, and on return it is warmed up for days.

Thin windows, thin walls. Heat leaves in winter, summer heat gets in. The bill grows both ways.

The Artemis house

Solar panels flush with the roof plane. Dark panels lie level with the flat roof, with a slim bronze edge along the rim. From the street the house looks clean, and the roof works all day.

Smart climate and lighting. The house holds its temperature itself and turns off unnecessary light. The system's job is to keep the house from cooling and going damp while the owner is away, and to have it warm on arrival.

Deep windows. Tall windows in slim bronze frames are set deep into the wall. In summer the reveal holds the shade; in winter the glass lets the low sun in. Plenty of light, little overheating.

A stone outline. A plinth of dark volcanic stone, stone terraces, light lime plaster. Stone heats slowly and cools slowly: the house holds an even temperature without swings.

A shared heated pool. Evenings in the north can be cool, and the heating keeps the pool usable all year round, in January too.

Why this matters for letting

Future tenants compare comfort and bills. Light, climate control and insulation affect the home and its owner’s costs. Rental use is not included in the investor-return calculation.

Why this matters for a shareholder

Quality and equipment shape a home’s appeal to buyers. Residence sales provide the receipts for investors’ cash exits. The model targets 9% annualised over 36 months. Investment model.

The north and the sun

On the northern slope the sun is softer than in the south, which is why it is not hot here and everything is green. There is enough light for panels in the Canaries all year round. By day the roof works; in the evening the house stays warm thanks to the stone, and the pool thanks to the heating.
