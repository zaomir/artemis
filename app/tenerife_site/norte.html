La Quinta, Santa Úrsula: the north of Tenerife, where people live

La Quinta is a residential neighbourhood in Santa Úrsula on Tenerife’s green northern slope. Puerto de la Cruz is 8 km away and Playa del Ancón around 3 km. Here we are selecting a plot for twenty Artemis residences. Home purchase and investment participation are described separately.

Spain: a European foundation for everyday life

Spain is part of the European Union, euro area and Schengen area. For a homeowner, that means payments in euros and Spain's property purchase process: a notary, the Land Registry, banks and local professionals to guide the transaction.

Tenerife is part of Spain. Life by the Atlantic comes with the infrastructure of a European country: healthcare, education, transport and communications.

Tenerife: eternal spring in a European country

Tenerife is known as the island of eternal spring. The Atlantic and the trade winds moderate summer and winter, keeping terrace living, coastal walks and outdoor sport part of everyday life throughout the year.

The Canaries offer a combination that is rare in a European country: a mild climate and life beside the ocean all year round. Northern Tenerife is greener, cloudier and more humid than the south; temperatures vary with altitude. This is the green north we have chosen for Artemis.

Infrastructure for year-round living

The island has two airports, the TF-1 and TF-5 roads, the TITSA bus network and fibre connectivity. Puerto de la Cruz is home to Hospiten Bellevue University Hospital, while Santa Cruz has major public hospitals.

Families can choose public and private schools, including British, German and French schools; people working here have internet and coworking spaces. These facilities are described in the Why Tenerife guide. Everyday life in La Quinta connects with Santa Úrsula, Puerto de la Cruz and La Orotava.

Services in English

Tenerife has English-speaking lawyers and activity companies offering several languages. English helps with arranging trips and professional support when buying a home. For a particular service, you can choose someone who speaks your language in advance; official documents are drawn up in Spanish.

A Central European community

Northern Tenerife has longstanding ties with Central Europe. German and Swiss families founded a school in Puerto de la Cruz in 1909; today, Deutsche Schule Tenerife continues to operate on the island.

International schools, local communities and services for foreign residents offer ways into a new environment. Children's schools, languages, sport and neighbours provide opportunities to meet people while staying connected to a European social circle.

Everyday life

Choose a place to live by its everyday journeys: shops, healthcare, schools and the trip into town. Try those routes and compare several island neighbourhoods before choosing a home.

Where it is on the map

Santa Úrsula lies between Puerto de la Cruz and the island's capital. La Quinta is its coastal part below the TF-5, on a slope that faces the Atlantic. Exit 29 leads off the TF-5 to the district.

North against south

The south is beach, heat, yellow dust and a holiday flow that changes every week. People fly there to rest.

The north is greenery, a mild day without heat, banana terraces, laurel forest on the slopes and Mount Teide above the clouds. People come here to live: the town is close, the capital is close, the airport is close. The surroundings are Western European.

If you have already looked at the south, you know what January looks like there: the same sun loungers and the same people with suitcases. In the north, January is a green slope, a “sea of clouds” above it at around 800–1 500 metres, and warm water in the heated pool in the evening.

La Quinta as a district

La Quinta is a residential area, not a holiday zone. People live here all year round; they do not check in for a week.

Neighbours live here year-round. Future owners should consider an ordinary weekday alongside the view from the terrace.

The twenty Artemis houses form a small, enclosed complex, not a tower block. One architectural language, Canary Stone: warm lime plaster, a plinth of dark volcanic stone, flat roofs with solar panels, tall windows in bronze frames, stone terraces.
