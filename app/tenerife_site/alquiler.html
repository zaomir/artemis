How a house at La Quinta earns after handover

Long-term letting in Santa Úrsula is 12–15 € per metre a month, and in neighbouring Puerto de la Cruz about 17 €. For a house of 72 metres that is 864–1 080 € a month. The dividend on shares in the developer company Artemis is calculated from the same long-term letting and is published as a percentage: an estimated 2,5–3,5 % a year before company profit tax.

Two owners, two incomes

The owner with the keys. Bought a house, or exchanged shares for metres, and lets it himself. His income is the rent less his costs.

The shareholder. Left the shares in the company. The company lets the metres that were not exchanged on long-term contracts, and the shareholder receives a dividend in euros on each share. He does not look for a tenant.

From rent to dividend

The dividend is calculated from long-term letting only. The formula is simple:

rent per metre a month × 12 − costs = rent of a metre a year after costs = the dividend per share before company profit tax.

With the numbers:

  • rent: the midpoint of the Santa Úrsula range, 13,5 € per metre a month;
  • over a year: 13,5 × 12 = 162 € per metre;
  • costs across seven lines: 46,3%, 75 € per metre;
  • what is left is the rent after costs, and the dividend is calculated from it: an estimated 2,5–3,5 % a year before company profit tax.

In the mild scenario, with the same 200 € of fees on a larger house of 75,5 m², management at 8% and repairs at 4%, costs are 41,8% and the estimate is about 94 € per share. In the strict one, with rent of 12 € per metre, 200 € of fees on a smaller house of 65,4 m², management at 12%, a month of vacancy and repairs at 6%, costs are 58,7% and the figure is about 59 €. The estimated dividend is 2,5–3,5 % a year before company profit tax.

Why long-term, not holiday letting

Under Ley 6/2025, new houses in the Canaries cannot be used for short-term holiday letting for ten years. The Artemis dividend is therefore built on long-term letting: a contract for a year or longer. A tenant on a contract pays every month, not from one stay to the next.

Three ways to live with a house

Live in it yourself. The simplest way to make a house pay is not to pay someone else's rent. You live at La Quinta for a season or a year.

Let it long term. A contract for a year or longer. In the north this is the calmest arrangement: people come here to live, not for a week at the beach.

Let it for a season. A contract for several months, for those who spend the winter on the island or work from home.

Who rents in the north

The north is rented by people who want greenery and a town rather than sun loungers: Puerto de la Cruz 8 km away, Tenerife Norte airport 15–20 minutes away, Santa Cruz 25–30 minutes away. They want a new house that is not cold in winter and not stuffy in summer.

The order of entry

Joining the waiting list is free. We are currently choosing the plot in the La Quinta area of Santa Úrsula and discussing the options with waiting-list members. We take no money before the plot is chosen: the investment agreement arrives when the majority of the waiting list approves the plot. Under the agreement you buy shares in the developer company at the price of the current step: one share is one metre. In full straight away: a 10% bonus; in instalments over six months: a 5% bonus; in instalments over a year: no bonus. Every week of construction: a report. After handover: a dividend in euros on each share from long-term letting less costs, estimated, before company profit tax, or an exchange of shares for the metres of your own residence.
